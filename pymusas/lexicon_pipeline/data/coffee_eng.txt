Coffee
The history of coffee
There are various stories about the discovery of coffee However many of them share one common feature shepherds and goats or sheep are involved Here are two of the most popular urban legends
An Abyssinian shepherd Kaldi grew anxious when his goatherd had not returned by dusk He found the animals eating berries of an unknown shrub To his surprise they danced and jumped around even though they were normally sound asleep at that time of the day The shepherd decided to take some berries with him and show them to the imam However the imam believed them to be a work of the devil and threw them into the campfire As a heavenly scent spread into the air the monks hurried to the place that it came from On seeing this the imam took back his words and decided to test the power of the berries The monks noticed that after having consumed the drink made of these berries they were able to stay awake better during prayer and meditation The news about the magic potion spread little by little over an increasingly wide area and eventually to the whole world
The second version of the same story tells about a shepherd who decides to try the berries for himself As the berries tasted bitter the shepherd tried soaking them in water but the taste did not improve Eventually he came up with the idea of trying to soften the taste by roasting Coffee had been invented
A totally different version is presented in the story of Omar a man who was expelled to the desert because of inappropriate conduct Omar was forced to rely on nature for his food and so ate berries from the bushes He noticed that the coffee berry in particular possessed refreshing qualities When his punishment was over Omar started to grow the coffee tree and was later canonized by coffee farmers
When searching for more authentic reasons one must resort to the help of experts of different fields Botanists have proved that coffee originally came from Mid-Ethiopia where it still grows wild today The first archaeological evidence of the use of coffee can be traced back 4000 years The yield of the coffee tree was not consumed as a drink Rather the berries were eaten raw The warriors mixed crushed berries into animal fat The mixture of caffeine fat and meat protein gave them additional strength to fight tribal wars The first signs of the use of the coffee tree as a raw material for a drink are quite interesting There is evidence to suggest that coffee was not consumed roasted and extracted for example but as an alcoholic drink The coffee berry contains plenty of sugar the darkening of the bean is caused by the burning of the sugar so it was easy to turn it into wine At the same time there is evidence to suggest that the preparation of coffee by extraction was beginning Both drinks were called qahwah which means the one that refreshes and makes the spirits rise At first the berries were boiled in water immediately after picking Later they were roasted on an open fire In the 13th century the beans were separated from the pulp and then dried in the sun to prolong their storage life The story of the coffee drink as we know it starts from this moment
The coffee plant
It says 100 % Arabica on the package of a better-quality coffee Does this mean that coffee has been brought from Arabia No not at all The text refers to the coffee plant used Another type of widely used coffee plant is called robusta in the vernacular Botanists call these varieties Coffea arabica and Coffea canephora
The coffee tree grows in a narrow zone in the equator It chooses its habitat carefully each variety requires a suitable altitude from sea level 101 rainfall and soil for successful cultivation
The seed is planted in sandy soil The growing roots push the seed above the ground causing the seed to separate from the stem and reveal two small leaves The young plant thrives in sunshine as long as it gets some shade every now and then for example from the leaves of trees It takes some time before the plant develops and one can not expect to produce fruit until the fourth year However the plant remains productive for up to twenty years The coffee tree does not have a special season but yields crop all the year round Because there are raw and ripe fruits hanging in the branches simultaneously hand-picking is the only possibility The ripening of the flower into a fruit takes several months The coffee tree requires abundant care Without pruning it could grow to the height of 20 metres owever the height is usually kept much lower to facilitate harvesting
Coffea arabica
The Arabica produces about 70 per cent of the world 's coffee Its popularity is due to its superior taste which in turn is due to its low caffeine content Arabica is considerably more demanding than robusta Its low resistance to diseases pests and frost makes cultivation difficult and also decreases the number of fruits that can be picked If it is to thrive arabica requires a mean temperature of 15-24 degrees and annual rainfalls of 150-200 ml The plant thrives best on the slopes of mountains especially in volcanic soil that is approximately 1-2 kilometres above sea level It will bloom after the rainy season but the crop can not be harvested for another nine months
Coffea canephora robusta
The name of this type of coffee refers not only to its taste but also to its resistance robust stands for something hard strong and powerful Although ideal growing conditions are temperatures of 25-30 degrees and a maximum growing height of 700 metres Robusta can thrive in hotter conditions closer to sea level It can also tolerate bigger rainfall variations than arabica
For example the plant is sturdy enough to withstand annual rainfalls of more than 3 metres and also periods of drought its roots are able to collect sufficient amounts of water for its survival in periods of drought In addition Robusta tolerates plagues better than arabica and yields a more abundant crop the ripening time is 10-11 months Robusta 's share of the coffee market is 30 % The reasons for its popularity include its cheaper price and double caffeine content compared to arabica
Picking and separation of the bean
The two beans or seeds must be separated from the pulp This is performed using either wet-processing or dry-processing The former is usually considered the best method Undoubtedly there are different opinions about this However wet-processing is certainly a considerably more expensive procedure
Evaluation
Before agreeing a deal a buyer wants to be confident about the quality of the merchandise in this case coffee Consequently a sample lot is taken from the bean sack and the beans are judged by weight size and appearance naturally without forgetting the taste of the coffee brewed from them The beans that do not qualify are calculated and the amount is compared to the size of the sample lot There are no worldwide classification standards so the quality of elite coffees of different countries may vary quite a lot However a standard for the size of beans does exist
Cupping
The taste of a particular coffee is checked by a procedure called cupping As more than one coffee is tasted at one time the same grind temperature of water infusing time size of cup and size and shape of spoon are used in order to ensure comparability A similar roast is selected either on the basis of the roasting time or roast degree
Most coffee packages in shops contain a mixture of several different coffee varieties In this way it is possible to produce a coffee in which every constituent brings out the best qualities of the others The biggest problem the coffee companies face is how to sustain a uniform quality the same result must be obtained year after year by combining different varieties of coffee
Roasting process
During the first few minutes of the roasting process the bean retains its original colour It then turns yellow and pale The accompanying scent of burning grass indicates that the moisture has begun to evaporate This is followed by a cracking sound which marks the beginning of the actual roasting The water continues to evaporate the sugars melt and finally the structures break releasing the oils This is a good indicator of the ripeness of the beans If a light roast is desired the roasting is interrupted after the initial crack If a dark roast is desired the roasting is continued The colour and scent of the beans reveal the right roast degree The sugar continues to melt and the temperature continues to rise causing a reaction with the oils until there is a second crack Crack The increasing smoke indicates that the sugar has begun to burn more intensely and that the coffee is becoming stronger The structure of the bean continues to break until the desired result is achieved However if all the sugar burns to the end the beans become useless
Roast degrees
There are many different roast degrees Generally speaking the darker the roast the stronger the taste A darker roast also contains fewer acids and less caffeine We have collected a few different classifications for you As you can see they tend to be quite different from one another For example how can Italian roast be a synonym for espresso roast if espresso roast is lighter than French roast but Italian roast is the synonym for French roast Our advice is not to buy a pig in a poke ask to be shown the colour of the beans before you buy
Grind degrees
The coarseness of the grind is selected on the basis of the production method and time As a rule of thumb the finer the grind the shorter the filtering time A finer grind guarantees a larger extraction area but sets higher demands for filtration In general there are four degrees
Turkish grind extremely finely ground dust-like
Espresso grind a fine grind almost dust-like powder
Drip grind the grind used for drip coffee the usual
Pot grind a coarse grind easy to separate from water
The text on the coffee packages that identifies the grind does not guarantee homogeneity There may be considerable dissimilarity between different companies This is probably caused by the divergences between different roast degrees and bean varieties the conceptual differences between manufacturers and the condition of the blades of coffee grinders One can notice this especially in connection with the espresso grind The best final result can be obtained by using one 's own coffee grinder
Storage of coffee
The taste of coffee does not improve with age unlike that of wine The deterioration of beans or of ground coffee can not be stopped but naturally it is possible to slow it down When choosing the storage method primary attention should be given to the amount and storage time of the coffee Unnecessary variations in temperature should also be avoided
General facts about the storage of coffee
Oxygen light and moisture are the worst enemies of roasted coffee Dark storage is not difficult to arrange but protecting the coffee from oxygen can be Coffee powder in particular should not be moved from one package to another as moving gives the coffee a thorough oxygen bath and eradicates the freshness Airtight packaging helps protect the coffee from the effects of oxygen and moisture
If the coffee is to be used within one month room temperature is the best storage solution In this way moving the coffee from one temperature to another can be avoided The old-fashioned jam jar that has been equipped with a washer and a shutter mechanism is an excellent storage container for opened coffee packages in particular The Mini-grip bag is good for storing beans The remaining air can be pressed out so that the taste of the coffee will not deteriorate Remember to close the bag carefully
The refrigerator may keep food and coffee cold but it is also full of odours drifting in the air that can contaminate particularly delicate aromas An opened can of tuna next to the coffee package is not a good idea As an air-tight environment is of the uttermost importance the refrigerator is an OK solution if the consumption of the coffee is low but regular
If the Coffee will not be used within one month it should be stored in the freezer Like the refrigerator the freezer is not an aroma-free environment Consequently you need to ensure that your coffee is packaged in airtight containers to ensure that external odours do not corrupt the taste Ground coffee can be frozen in its own unopened package but coffee beans should be put into small Mini-grip bags Before using coffee that has been frozen you should allow it to warm to room temperature in its airtight packaging Exposing icy coffee to warm indoor air causes the moisture in the coffee to condense and the coffee to deteriorate Coffee that has been taken from the freezer should not be refrozen
Preparing coffee
Coffee can be prepared in many different ways The different preparation methods are equally valid producing excellent variations of coffee with their own particular nuances
Pot coffee
The simplest method of preparing coffee is pot coffee This method produces strong deep brown coffee
Measure the right amount of water into a pot A coffee cup is the best measure Note that it is worth preparing a little more coffee as an unusable mixture of water and grounds will always remain at the bottom of the pot
Bring to the boil and add the coffee powder The quantity of coffee that you add will be dependent on the taste you desire but one coffee measure per cup is a good starting point
Bring to the boil Do not cook
Depending on the grind degree leave to rest for 3-5 minutes The pot grind is the best when using the drip grind plenty of grounds can end up on the bottom of the cups Remember the finer the grind the shorter the extracting time
Pour out the grounds from the surface and then pour the clear coffee into cups Be careful not to mix in the grounds that have sunk to the bottom of the pot
French press
The French press is an inexpensive way to prepare good coffee The cooking apparatus consists of three parts a glass jug and a lid with a plunger The plunger is a metal net at the end of the handle which settles tightly against the edges of the jug
Put the water pot to heat on the stove Measure an appropriate amount of coffee powder into a preheated dry jug pot grind recommended When the water is almost at boiling point pour it into the jug
Mix the coffee powder in the water with a wooden spoon and leave to settle for 3-5 minutes depending on the coarseness of the grind Put the lid on top of the glass jug but leave the filter part up
At the appropriate time press the filter disk down Be careful when doing so as even a small movement sideways can break the glass vessel
Pour the coffee into cups and serve immediately
Automatic drip brewer
The drip coffee method is the easiest of the brewing methods One does not need to worry about the filtration time or separating the grounds from the water as gravity takes care of the right filtration time and filter paper ensures a clean final result Indeed all one needs to do with a cleaned brewer is to fill the water tank with fresh water measure the coffee to the paper filter and then switch on the power The brewer takes care of the rest
That said it 's important to fill the water tank with the right amount of water Do not trust the scale on the apparatus especially if the measure is a coffee cup as opposed to a decilitre As the size and shape of coffee cups can vary considerably the best cup measure is the coffee cup that you will be using Remember fresh coffee especially absorbs water like a sponge 2 millilitres per gram of coffee e.g. over one decilitre of absorbed water This means that approximately 60 grams of coffee powder is needed for one litre of coffee So if in doubt include a little extra water in the water tank
Measure the right amount of coffee powder into the filter Use the drip grind if the cooking apparatus utilizes a paper filter If a metal or plastic durable filter is utilized use a coarser grind This should prevent a lot of the grounds from settling at the bottom of the cup
Level the coffee in the filter so that the filtering will take place steadily and evenly Failure to do so will result in thin coffee as the water will flow through the thinnest section The best way to level the coffee is with a coffee measure or your fingertips Tapping the sides of the filter may appear to level the coffee powder but it does not do so
Do not leave the coffee on the heat disk for longer than twenty minutes Serve immediately or pour it into a vacuum jug
Percolator
Making coffee with a percolator is similar in principle to making coffee in an espresso pot However the coffee neither percolates because of the pressure nor stays in the upper vessel Nowadays most percolators are electronically operated heating models However there are still some models that are heated on the stove
Fill the container with fresh cold water When measuring pay attention to the amount of water that will be absorbed by the coffee grounds Measure the right amount of pot grind to the filter vessel and cover it with the perforated lid Then close the percolator lid
Switch the percolator on or if it is heated on the stove put it on the stove As the water begins to boil it will rise up the middle pipe and flow through the perforated lid onto the coffee powder After percolation the liquid coffee will begin to drop to the same place from which the water originally rose Electronically operated percolators usually reduce the power of the resistance as soon as the water has made a full circle If this function is not included or if the device is manual the user needs to do this Switch off the device or remove it from the stove If this is not done the coffee will begin to boil rise up along the pipe again and percolate for a second time If this happens the coffee will be destroyed In many percolators there is a partly transparent lid through which the progress of the percolation can be observed
Vac pot
Vac pot is by far the most interesting way of preparing coffee The procedure is based on heat expansion and heat shrinkage The cooking apparatus is almost completely made of glass which makes it possible to follow preparation The water can be heated in many different ways Some of the cooking apparatuses are heated on the stove some with alcohol and some on a separate electric resistance
Fill the lower container with water and fix the top in place Be careful with the middle pipe Measure the coffee powder to the upper container The best grind coarseness for the vac pot is somewhere between the drip grind and the pot grind
Light the alcohol or switch on the resistances Heat expansion will cause the water to begin to boil and then rise along the middle pipe to the upper container Note that part of the water will remain in the lower container as the pipe does not reach all the way to the bottom
Once most of the water has risen up switch off the heat The water will still continue to rise for a while Stir the coffee powder into the water
When the air of the lower container begins to cool a vacuum will form The middle pipe will then absorb the coffee back from the upper container as a means of replacing the partial vacuum in the lower container The grounds will remain in the upper container
Remove the upper container and the middle pipe The coffee should be served immediately When you prepare coffee with a vac pot you will be rewarded by admiring and astonished looks from your guests as well as by excellent coffee
