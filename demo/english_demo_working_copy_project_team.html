ABOUT THE PUBLIC DATA

COVID-19 metaphors make the method inspectable.

#ReframeCovid is a crowdsourced, multilingual collection of alternatives to war language and other ways of framing COVID-19. This demo uses its English entries because they may be redistributed. It is not a patient cohort and does not validate a cancer Menu. The extraction, checks, categories and ranking were added by this project—not by the original dataset publishers.

  1. 600 quality-filtered public posts from two COVID illness communities on Reddit (March 2020 - July 2021), split into 1,823 passages, with items of known status hidden among them: 17 published Metaphor Menu entries and 130 CC-licensed #ReframeCovid entries as known-good, and 100 figurative disease mentions about other topics as known-bad probes.
  2. A local model family proposed metaphor candidates from every passage. A second model family independently checked whether each proposed phrase was metaphorical in its passage; 1,665 verified candidates went on to screening.
  3. The workflow then recorded separate outputs for metaphor validity and illness relevance, lived experience, and resemblance to published Metaphor Menu entries (score 0-10).
  4. Overlapping spans of the same expression were merged, and the four planted entries quoted inside the scoring prompt were removed: 1,490 ranked candidates, of which this demo shows the 415 with open-licensed text (Reddit text is not redistributed).
  5. This page: the full ranked list, all 415 candidates, with every screen verdict and filter.

What “planted” means

The rows marked PLANTED check item · Menu entry are not from this corpus. They are published entries of the Metaphor Menu (Lancaster University; 17 entries), inserted into the pool as a known-good check. They went through the same extraction as everything else, so if the ranking works they should surface near the top — in this list the 13 planted entries that yield a candidate sit at median position 3 of 415. A further 4 planted entries are not considered and were removed before ranking, because they are quoted as examples inside the scoring prompt and would be scored partly against themselves. Planted items show where validated expressions land; they do not prove that the unvalidated candidates below them are well ordered.
